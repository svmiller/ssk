title:  "What Time Expressions Get the 에 Particle?"
date:   2026-11-25
author: steve
---


In Korean, the particle 에 attaches to a time expression when you want to say when an event or action takes place. This is especially common with specific points in time, such as clock times, days of the week, dates, months, years, and parts of the day. For example, 7시에 means "at seven o’clock," 월요일에 means "on Monday", and 아침에 means "in the morning." When several time expressions are combined, 에 usually comes at the end. For example, 월요일 아침 7시에  would be "at 7 on Monday morning." 

However, some common time words are never paired with 에 (for which I'm aware). These include words such as 오늘 (today), 어제 (yesterday), 내일 (tomorrow), 지금 (now), and 매일 (every day). Think of these as self-contained time adverbs: they already tell you when something happens or happened, so they generally don't need an additional particle. From an English speaker's perspective, I think you can anticipate most of these.

There is some flexibility with broader expressions such as 작년 (last year) or 다음 주 (next week), which can take 에 in certain sentences. For example, "I'm studying Korean this month" (이번 달 한국어를 공부해요) wouldn't get a time particle because the time is not a destination, of sorts, and the action is implicitly ongoing. "I'm studying Korean next week" (다음 주에 한국어를 공부해요) *would* get a time particle because it's a future event to take place at that time. I *think* you can anticipate a lot of these, though I don't doubt I'll screw these up from time to time.
