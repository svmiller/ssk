A native English speaker, I'm inclined to think of adjectives and verbs as two different parts of speech. Verbs are necessary for complete sentences. Adjectives, which describe nouns, are not. However, Koreans don't quite have the same concept of "adjectives" as we do, at least not as a completely separate part of speech. In Korean, what English speakers think of as adjectives are grammatically classified as [descriptive verbs (형용사)](https://www.90daykorean.com/korean-adjectives/). Unlike English, where an adjective such as cold or beautiful describes a noun and generally requires a linking verb, Korean descriptive verbs function as predicates in their own right. For example, an English speaker might see a sentence like "it is beautiful" and see 1) technically "beautiful" is not necessary for this sentence and 2) "beautiful" is linked to it. In Korean, it's a bit different. "Beautiful" is linked to "is."

These words will generally end in the familiar 다, which is the most common Korean equivalent to the English infinitive. Not all of them do, though. Here's a list of ones we learned.

| **English** | **Korean, Unconjugated** | **Polite present tense** |
|:-----------:|:------------------------:|:------------------------:|
| Hot | 덥다 | 더워요 |
| Cold | 춥다 | 추워요 |
| Clear | 맑다 | 맑아요 |
| Cloudy |	흐리다 | 흐려요 |
| Interesting | 재미있다 | 재미있어요 |
| Heavy | 무겁다 | 무거워요 |
| Light (as in weight) | 가볍다 | 가벼워요 |
| Easy | 쉽다 | 쉬워요 |
| Difficult | 어렵다 | 어려워요 |
| Spicy | 맵다 | 매워요 |
| Cute | 귀엽다 | 귀여워요 |
| Beautiful | 아름답다 | 아름다워요 |
| Frightening/Scary | 무섭다 | 무서워요 |


A couple of useful observations:

- Several of these use the ㅂ irregular conjugation: 덥다, 춥다, 무겁다, 가볍다, 쉽다, 어렵다, 맵다, 귀엽다, 아름답다, and 무섭다. The ㅂ changes to 우 or 오 when followed by certain vowel endings. That's why, for example, 춥다 becomes 추워요 rather than 춥어요. I understand these are useful descriptions to know at a beginner level, but it is a bit unusual that the ones we learned are paired with the need to understand the irregular ㅂ conjugation.
- 재미있다 is an interesting exception to the general pattern. It uses 있다, but functions as a descriptive predicate: literally something like "to have interest/fun," meaning "to be interesting" or "to be fun." I'm used to these having the 하다 suffix. This one doesn't.
